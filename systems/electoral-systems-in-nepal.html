Electoral Systems in Nepalनेपालमा निर्वाचन प्रणाली

Systems and institutionsप्रणाली र संस्थाहरू

Nepal uses a mixed parallel electoral system that combines first past the post voting in single member constituencies with closed list proportional representation.नेपालमा मिश्रित निर्वाचन प्रणाली छ। यसमा प्रत्यक्ष निर्वाचन र समानुपातिक निर्वाचन दुवै हुन्छन्।

The mixed system was adopted in the 2015 Constitution for both the House of Representatives and the provincial assemblies, at a 60 percent direct and 40 percent proportional split. Voters receive separate ballots for the two methods. For the House, the whole country is a single proportional constituency and voters choose a party, not an individual, on that ballot. For a provincial assembly, the province is the proportional constituency.नेपालमा मिश्रित समानान्तर प्रणाली छ: प्रत्यक्ष र समानुपातिक भाग छुट्टाछुट्टै चल्छन्।

The two parts run side by side, so a party's proportional seats do not compensate for its constituency results. This is a parallel system, not a compensatory one. Proportional seats are allocated using the Sainte-Lague method, with Nepal reported to use 1.4 as the first divisor instead of 1, a change intended to make a single seat harder for very small parties to win. Whether the 1.4 first divisor remains in the current election law and directive is To confirm, as is the exact current divisor sequence and the treatment of remainders.प्रतिनिधि सभामा समानुपातिक सिट पाउन दलले कम्तीमा ३ प्रतिशत मत ल्याउनुपर्छ। प्रदेश सभामा यो सीमा १.५ प्रतिशत हो।

Federal proportional threshold: a party must win at least 3 percent of total valid proportional votes to receive proportional seats in the House of Representatives. Provincial proportional threshold: a party must win at least 1.5 percent of total valid votes in the province to receive proportional seats in that provincial assembly. Proportional lists are closed lists submitted by parties. Voters vote for a party, and seats go to candidates in the order set by the party, subject to inclusion rules.Federal proportional threshold: a party must win at least 3 percent of total valid proportional votes to receive proportional seats in the House of Representatives. Provincial proportional threshold: a party must win at least 1.5 percent of total valid votes in the province to receive proportional seats in that provincial assembly. Proportional lists are closed lists submitted by parties. Voters vote for a party, and seats go to candidates in the order set by the party, subject to inclusion rules.

National party status is distinct from proportional seats. To be recognized as a national party, a party must also win at least one first past the post seat in the House, in addition to crossing the 3 percent proportional threshold. A party that crosses 3 percent but wins no direct seat still receives proportional seats, but is not recognized as a national party. This distinction is reported in a 2026 seat allocation explainer citing the House of Representatives Member Election Act, 2017 and the Political Parties Act, 2017.National party status is distinct from proportional seats.
