Inclusion Quotas and Proportional Representationसमावेशी प्रतिनिधित्व

Systems and institutionsप्रणाली र संस्थाहरू

Nepal's Constitution requires proportional and inclusive participation in state bodies, put into practice mainly through closed proportional representation lists and a one third women rule.नेपालको संविधानले सबै समूहको समावेशी सहभागिता खोज्छ। समानुपातिक सूचीमा महिला, दलित, जनजाति र अन्य समूहको प्रतिनिधित्व हुनुपर्छ।

The inclusion principle runs through the Constitution, including Article 18, which permits special provisions by law for disadvantaged groups, and Article 42, which gives socially, economically, or educationally backward groups the right to participate in state bodies on the principle of proportional inclusion. Article 42(1) names groups including women, Dalit, indigenous nationalities (Aadibasi Janajati), Madhesi, Tharu, Muslims, backward classes, minorities, marginalized communities, persons with disabilities, gender and sexual minorities, farmers, labourers, oppressed citizens, citizens of backward regions, and indigent Khas Arya.संविधानले राज्यका निकायमा समानुपातिक र समावेशी सहभागिता खोज्छ।

For elections, Articles 84 and 176 turn this into list rules and a women floor for the House and provincial assemblies. House closed lists, under Article 84(2) to (3), must ensure representation from women, Dalit, indigenous peoples, Khas Arya, Madhesi, Tharu, Muslims, and backward regions, on the basis of population, with geographical and territorial balance, and must include persons with disabilities. At least one third of the total members elected from each party in the Federal Parliament must be women, under Article 84(8), and provincial assemblies have a similar closed list rule and the same one third women rule under Article 176.प्रत्येक दलका निर्वाचित सदस्यमध्ये कम्तीमा एक तिहाइ महिला हुनुपर्छ। समानुपातिक सूचीमा महिला, दलित, जनजाति, मधेशी, थारू, मुस्लिम र पिछडिएका क्षेत्रको प्रतिनिधित्व हुनुपर्छ।

National Assembly seats, under Article 86, must include in each province's eight seats at least three women, one Dalit, and one person with a disability or from a minority community. Of the three nominated members, at least one must be a woman.
