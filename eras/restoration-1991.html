Restoration of Democracy and the 1991 Electionप्रजातन्त्र पुनर्स्थापना र सन् १९९१ को निर्वाचन

Eras and conceptsयुग र अवधारणाहरू

A popular movement in early 1990 ended the partyless Panchayat system and restored multiparty democracy, leading to a new constitution and the first general election under it in May 1991.सन् १९९० को जनआन्दोलनपछि पञ्चायत व्यवस्था अन्त्य भयो र बहुदलीय प्रजातन्त्र फर्कियो। सन् १९९१ मा नयाँ संविधानअनुसार आम निर्वाचन भयो।

Electionनिर्वाचन12 May 1991, 205 seats, Nepali Congress 110१२ मे १९९१, २०५ सिट, नेपाली कांग्रेस ११०

In February 1990, the Nepali Congress and left parties, united in part under the United Left Front, launched strikes and demonstrations in major cities. After severe police action and large protests, King Birendra capitulated in April 1990, dissolved the Panchayat system, lifted the ban on political parties, and released political prisoners. The ban lifting date of 8 April 1990 in one detailed account is To confirm for exact proclamation date.सन् १९९० को जनआन्दोलनले पञ्चायत व्यवस्था अन्त्य गर्यो र बहुदलीय प्रजातन्त्र पुनर्स्थापित भयो।

An interim government headed by Krishna Prasad Bhattarai was sworn in on 19 April 1990, with members from the Nepali Congress, communist parties, royal appointees, and independents. That government drafted the Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal, 1990, promulgated in November 1990, with one source giving 9 November 1990, which established Nepal as a parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarch, with fundamental rights and adult franchise.सन् १९९१ को निर्वाचनमा नेपाली कांग्रेसले ११० सिट जित्यो र गिरिजाप्रसाद कोइराला प्रधानमन्त्री बने।

The election was held on 12 May 1991 for 205 House seats, all elected by first past the post from 205 constituencies. The Nepali Congress won 110 seats and formed the government under Girija Prasad Koirala as prime minister, with CPN (UML) as the main opposition. Candidate total of 1,345, including 80 independents, as reported in one election overview, is To confirm against the Election Commission record. International observers characterized the elections as free and fair.
