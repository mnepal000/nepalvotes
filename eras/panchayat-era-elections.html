Panchayat Era Elections (1981 and 1986)पञ्चायतकालीन निर्वाचन

Eras and conceptsयुग र अवधारणाहरू

Under the partyless Panchayat system, political parties were banned and candidates stood as individuals. The 1981 and 1986 elections were direct elections to the national Panchayat legislature by universal adult suffrage.पञ्चायत व्यवस्थामा दलहरू प्रतिबन्धित थिए। सन् १९८१ र १९८६ मा राष्ट्रिय पञ्चायतका लागि प्रत्यक्ष निर्वाचन भयो।

The Panchayat system was established by the Constitution of 1962, proclaimed by King Mahendra after the 1960 takeover, with councils at village, district, and national levels under the king. The national body was the Rastriya Panchayat (National Panchayat). Early Panchayat legislatures were chosen indirectly.पञ्चायत व्यवस्थामा दल प्रतिबन्धित थिए र उम्मेदवार स्वतन्त्र रूपमा उभिन्थे।

After pressure for reform, King Birendra held a national referendum on 2 May 1980 on keeping a reformed Panchayat system or returning to multiparty democracy. The Panchayat option won with about 54.7 percent, with one source saying about 55 percent. The promised reforms were put into the third amendment to the 1962 Constitution in May 1981, keeping partylessness but introducing direct election of Rastriya Panchayat members by universal adult suffrage, and making the prime minister selected by the Rastriya Panchayat.सन् १९८१ र १९८६ मा राष्ट्रिय पञ्चायतका लागि प्रत्यक्ष निर्वाचन भयो। सन् १९९० मा पञ्चायत व्यवस्था अन्त्य भयो।

The 1981 election was the first direct election to the Rastriya Panchayat, and the 1986 election was the second and last direct election to it. In both, 112 seats were elected and 28 were appointed by the king, out of 140 total seats, as shown on the election pages. Turnout figures differ between sources: v1 election pages use 52.2 percent for 1981 and 60.3 percent for 1986, while a research draft reported 63 percent for 1981 and 60 percent for 1986. Those turnout figures are therefore To confirm against the Election Commission record, and this article does not settle them. Women elected were reported as two women in 1981, Nanimaiya Dahal (Kathmandu) and Bhadrakumari Ghale (Sindhuli), and three women in 1986, Sharda Malla (Dhanusha), Laxmi Singh (Sarlahi), and Chanda Shah (Bara), as reported from Election Commission data. Spellings are To confirm against the official record.
